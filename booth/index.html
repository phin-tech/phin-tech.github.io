Booth

Record narration against a demoreel video, with the script beside it.

demoreel renders a demo as silent scene videos and keeps each scene's narration in demo.json. Booth is where you read it aloud: the video plays, the script scrolls beside it, and what you record is kept as a take the scene can use.

Install #

brew install --cask phin-tech/tap/booth

Or download the zip from the latest release. Booth updates itself. It needs macOS 14 Sonoma or later and a microphone.

Open a project #

Open Project… (⌘O) takes a demoreel project folder, the one with demo.json in it. Render the scenes first with demoreel export: Booth plays each scene's stretch of the film it writes to out/ (or, after demoreel export --scenes, the scene's own video in out/scenes/), and reads each scene's length and what happens in it from out/manifest.json. Booth reloads by itself when an export finishes, and Reload (⌘R) picks up an edited demo.json.

A take changes how long its scene lasts, so export again after picking one. Render (⇧⌘R) runs demoreel's export on the project at draft quality (its menu has full quality). Booth uses demoreel from Homebrew (brew install phin-tech/tap/demoreel) when it finds it, and offers to install it the first time it starts without one: with Homebrew when the Mac has it, else from GitHub into ~/Library/Application Support/Booth/demoreel (that needs git, Node 22 or newer and ffmpeg, and says which is missing). Settings has Install and Update for a demoreel Booth installed, or you can point it at a checkout of your own. Booth records, cuts and edits scripts without demoreel; only Render needs it. Or leave demoreel watch <folder> running in a terminal, which exports whenever a take is picked.

Where the narration lives #

A project keeps each scene's narration in demo.json, or, once it has a script.md, there: a ## <scene name> heading per scene, then a paragraph per chunk, to write and edit as prose in any editor. File › Keep Narration in script.md moves it there (and out of demo.json), and a rough take with no script does it by itself. From then on, everything that changes narration in Booth (the script editor, cutting and joining scenes, an agent) writes script.md, and Booth reloads when you save it elsewhere. Booth gives each scene a short ref in demo.json and puts it at the end of its heading, ## intro · 01 · 0:00 – 0:07 {#k7qx}, so a scene keeps its narration when it is renamed, renumbered or shares its name. When Booth changes the script it changes only the lines it is about: your wrapping, comments and notes stay. A heading that matches no scene shows a warning. The rules are in demoreel's spec/, which Booth's tests run. File › Write Captions writes script.vtt: what the takes in use say, and when, along the whole video. demoreel reads script.md the same way.

The script, beside the picture #

Pick a scene and its narration appears as a teleprompter, split into the chunks demoreel cut it into. A marker sits on the word to say now. What happens on screen (a bullet appears, a click, a key) is shown as a tag above its chunk and as a dot on the timeline, so you know where to pause.

The video and the teleprompter can each open in a window of their own (⌥⌘V and ⌥⌘T), and ⌥⌘L moves the teleprompter between beside the video and below it.

It follows you, not a clock #

With Follow my voice on, Booth listens through the speech recogniser on your Mac and moves the marker to where you are. A misheard word, a skipped word or a word said twice does not lose your place, and nothing is sent anywhere. With it off, the marker advances at the pace you set, in words per minute. Fit pace to scene length sets that pace so the script takes as long as the scene does.

Takes #

Press record and read. Every take is kept as audio/<scene>-take-<n>.wav in the project, and the takes drawer lists them with how each compares with the scene: fits, with so many seconds spare, short, or so many seconds over. Tick the one the scene uses; the choice is kept in audio/takes.json, for demoreel to read, and demo.json is not touched.

Retake a chunk, not the whole scene #

When one sentence went wrong, Retake it. Booth records just that chunk and puts it into the take, and keeps the original to play beside it. Where another take got a chunk right, borrow it into the take the scene uses. Where each chunk ends is found from the words recognised in the finished take; when it has to be estimated from the pauses instead, the drawer says so and asks you to listen to verify the cuts.

Or have a voice read it #

Booth can read the narration for you, on your Mac, with an open-source voice engine. Install one or more in Settings › Voices:

Engine Like Download
Kokoro Small and quick, with eleven American and British voices. Reads plainly. 0.4 GB
Chatterbox Turbo Natural and quick, one voice. Acts on [laugh], [chuckle], [sigh], [gasp], [cough], [groan], [sniff], [shush] and [clear throat]. 0.9 GB
Orpheus The most expressive, and the slowest, with eight voices. Acts on [laugh], [chuckle], [sigh], [cough], [sniffle], [groan], [yawn] and [gasp]. 2 GB

The first one also installs a Python runtime they share (about 1.3 GB, with uv; Booth fetches uv too if you don't have it). After that they work offline. Everything goes in ~/Library/Application Support/Booth/Voices, and removing the last engine removes all of it. They need Apple silicon, and aren't in the App Store version.

The Generate button beside Record (a waveform with a plus) reads the scene with the engine and voice you pick, and files it as a take like any other (the Voice menu does the same with each engine's chosen voice). Booth knows exactly where each chunk ends in it, so you can retake its chunks and comp it, and regenerate one chunk (the waveform button beside Retake) without reading the rest again. A regenerated chunk can go into a recorded take too: it is converted to that take's sample rate and channels. Borrowing a chunk between a generated take and a recorded one doesn't work, as they are recorded differently (24 kHz mono against your microphone's format); regenerate it there instead.

Voice › Generate All Scenes with … reads every scene with narration, one after another, each into a new take that becomes the one the scene uses (the takes there before are kept, so you can switch back). Its progress shows at the bottom of the window, with a button to stop it.

A generated take is marked in the takes drawer with the engine that read it (hover for the voice), and a chunk read again by a voice has a waveform on its version pill. Booth keeps which voice read what in <take>.voice.json beside the take; demoreel ignores it.

A word a voice reads wrong can be spelled out for it in demo.json: "say": { "demoreel": "demo reel" }. Only the voices get the spelling; the script, the teleprompter and the captions keep the word as written.

The narration's [audio tags] go to the engine that understands them, written its way; tags it doesn't know are left out, as they are for Kokoro, so [enthusiasm] is never read aloud.

Edit the line where you stumbled on it #

⌘E turns the teleprompter into a script editor. Saving writes the new narration into demo.json for that scene and changes nothing else in the file: your keys stay in the order you wrote them, and your indentation stays as it was.

Start from a video of your own #

File › Record Screen… (⌃⌘R) records it for you: pick a window or a whole display in macOS's own picker (Booth needs no Screen Recording permission for that), and do what you want to show. A red ● with the time sits in the menu bar while it records; click it, or choose File › Stop Recording Screen, and Booth asks where the project goes and makes it around the recording, as below. With Record My Voice with the Screen ticked in the File menu, the microphone is recorded too: talk as you go, and what you said becomes the script, split into chunks where you paused, ready to cut and record properly. Untick it to record the screen alone and narrate afterwards.

File › New Project from Video… (⇧⌘N) makes a project around any video: Booth copies it into the project's media/ folder and writes a demo.json with one full-frame scene, its whole length. Add Video to Project… puts another one after the scenes there are. Booth plays the stretch of video itself, so there is nothing to render first.

Then work in either order:

  • Cut first, then record. Move the playhead to where a scene should end and press Cut at Playhead (⌘K) on the timeline. Write each scene's narration and record it as usual.
  • Dub it rough, then edit the script. Record over the video with no script and say whatever fits: what you said becomes each scene's narration, in script.md in the project (a heading per scene, a paragraph per chunk), and script.vtt has what you said and when, along the whole video. Edit script.md in any editor: a blank line starts the next chunk, and lines without one between them are one chunk however they wrap. Booth reloads when you save it, so your edits are the script to record properly or have a voice read.
  • Mark as it plays. Press M at each place a new scene should start, as the video plays or while you record over it. Each press drops a mark (blue scissors) on the timeline, a quarter second earlier for the time a press takes, and on a shot change or pause that Find Cuts found within half a second. Scrub to check one; its menu moves it to the playhead or dismisses it. Cut at Marks (the scissors with a count) then cuts at all of them at once. The key can be changed in Settings.
  • Record first, then cut. Record over the whole video. With no script, what you said becomes the narration, split into sentences where you paused. Then cut it: each chunk goes to the scene it was said in, and every take is cut at the quietest moment near the cut, so the scenes keep their takes.

Find Cuts (the wand) looks for shot changes in the picture and long pauses in the voice, on your Mac, and marks them on the timeline with scissors: click one to cut there or dismiss it. Join Next puts a scene and the one after it back together, with their narration and the takes they use joined. Nothing recorded is lost: a take that was cut is kept whole, with its retakes, in audio/set-aside/, as are the other takes of a scene joined into the one before. Cutting or joining renumbers the scenes after it, and their takes are renamed to match.

Cutting never touches the video file: each scene is a stretch of it. Each scene is a clip with from, to and layout: "full" in demo.json, which demoreel renders as that stretch of the video, full frame (it needs the demoreel that reads them).

Let an agent help #

Booth can be driven by an agent, such as Claude Code, over MCP. It can read the scenes, look at frames of the video and at what was said in a take, suggest cuts (they appear on the timeline for you to take or leave), cut and join scenes, have a voice read a scene, and suggest new narration: a line that runs too long, a transcript to tidy, a cue to say as it happens. A suggested script shows above the teleprompter with the changed words marked and the agent's reason, and Use It saves it; it also says which chunks of the takes you have will need retaking. Recording stays with you.

To add it to Claude Code, copy the command from Settings › Agents; it is

claude mcp add booth -- /Applications/Booth.app/Contents/MacOS/booth-mcp

booth-mcp starts Booth if it isn't running and works on the project open in it, on this Mac only. Not in the App Store version.

Settings #

Settings › Updates turns automatic update checks on and off, and Get beta versions adds the beta channel. Settings › Voices installs and removes the voice engines, and picks each one's voice. Settings › Agents turns the MCP server on and off, and has the command to give an agent.
