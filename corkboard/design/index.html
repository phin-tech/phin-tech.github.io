Corkboard's design

What was settled before the first line of code, and why. Words are as CONTEXT.md defines them; the decisions that are hard to reverse are in docs/adr/.

What it's for #

A better Linear client, for shaping work rather than only seeing it: better Filters and Search, Links between Issues and from Issues to Projects that are quick to make, Sub-issues that are easy to manage, and Projects that are easy to shape and read. Speed and the keyboard are how it gets there. Initiatives, roadmaps and Cycles stay in Linear's app for now.

Keys #

Red Pen's model, with Microfiche's selection:

  • No modes for moving: j k, g g G, J K between Issues even in the preview, ↩ shows the preview, Tab moves the keys into it, f shows an Issue over the whole window, q or ⎋ steps out.
  • Verbs are two-key mnemonics, as Red Pen's s l and m w are: l p link to a Parent, l b blocking, l r related, l d a duplicate, p p into a Project, s … snooze, u undo. Space or , is the Leader menu, with every Command; ⌘K is the palette. The leader key can be another, in Settings › Shortcuts (leader_key); every key will be rebindable there later.
  • A Selection is a range (V, then j k) or single Issues (v), and the two combine. A verb acts on the Selection, or on the Issue under the cursor with none. The status bar says how many are selected.
  • The mouse reaches the same Commands, with no mode to switch: a click moves the cursor, ⌘-click picks (v), ⇧-click makes a range (V), a double click or a click on the cursor's row shows the preview, and a right click has the verbs for what's chosen (a row outside it becomes the cursor first, as in Finder). The title bar has buttons for the commonest, each saying its key, and pointing at an identifier names the row's keys.
  • A verb that needs a target opens a picker: fuzzy over identifiers and titles from the Local copy, offering only targets that make sense (no Parent that would make a loop).

Places #

The sidebar has My Issues, Inbox, Triage, Projects and Saved Searches (⌘1 to ⌘9).

  • Projects open as an Outline. The moves are Microfiche's: >> Nests under the Issue above, << takes it out a level, [e ]e reorder, m Places it elsewhere while showing where, z a folds. Rows show the Workflow state, how many Sub-issues are done (3/5) and blockers. Nesting moves an Issue into its Parent's Project and Milestone, never its Team, and the status bar says so (WEB-40 → Q1 Platform).
  • Built: the sidebar lists only the Projects favorited (with its +, or Project › Favorite or Unfavorite), kept in the settings file as favorite_projects, and g p picks any one. Sub-issues are ordered by Linear's subIssueSortOrder, top-level Issues by sortOrder. m (Placing) and dragging to reorder or Nest are still to come.
  • The Project heads its Outline: its Summary, Project status (w s), Lead (w l), start and Target dates (w ⇧S, w t), how much is done, and its description; w r renames it and w m writes the Summary. Every Milestone is headed, empty ones too, with its Target date and how much of it is done. ⇧M n makes one after the cursor's, and ⇧M r, ⇧M t, ⇧M k ⇧M j and ⇧M d rename, date, move and delete the cursor's, or one picked when the cursor is under none. Deleting one leaves its Issues under No milestone, and u brings it back with them (ADR 0005). An Issue dragged onto a Milestone's heading, or onto a row under it, moves into that Milestone as p m would, bringing the Selection when it's part of it; one outside the Project stays put.
  • Every other list is flat; z a opens an Issue's Sub-issues in place.
  • Inbox: one row for each Issue. s … snoozes and d archives, in Linear; opening one marks it read. Every Issue verb works from its preview.
  • Built: g i shows the Inbox, its unread count in the sidebar; each sync brings every Notification of the last three months that isn't archived. A row says who did what to which Issue and when, unread ones with a dot. ↩ (or a second click) shows the Issue in the preview and marks the row read; ⇧U marks it read and x archives it (d tears off a Sticky), each a Change sent as notificationUpdate's readAt or notificationArchive, and u takes it back. One about an Issue the Local copy hasn't synced opens in Linear. corkboard inbox and the MCP server's list_notifications list what's unread. Snoozing is still to come; one snoozed in Linear stays hidden until it wakes.
  • Triage: a accepts into the Team's default state, A accepts with a line of fields in Capture's syntax, x declines with a reason posted as a comment, l d marks a duplicate, s … snoozes. All work on a Selection.

Modes #

Whatever's shown can be seen three ways, and the verbs are the same in each. The title bar's switch shows which, and clicks between them, as their keys do.

  • List mode, rows, which Corkboard opens in.
  • Corkboard mode (b), cards on a cork board, with Strings for Links. ⌘= and ⌘- zoom it, as =, -, ⌘-scroll and a pinch do, and ⌘0 or 0 puts it back; elsewhere ⌘= and ⌘- are the text size. Dragging the bare cork moves around it. Dragging from a card's tack to another card ties a String, and a picker asks which Link it makes, worded from the first card: blocks, blocked by, related, duplicate, Parent or Sub-issue.
  • Paper mode (P), the Issues written out as a page, for reading down a Project or a Filter as text: a bullet journal on graph paper, or a Microfiche Note. View › Switch Paper Style (space v s) and Settings choose. Headings group them as the board's columns do (or by Milestone, on a Project); Sub-issues go beneath their Parents even where their state would put them under another Heading, and say their own state; Links are Fields named from the Issue's side. It moves as Microfiche's normal mode does, and there's no insert mode: nothing on the paper is text to edit. Each Project and Filter keeps its own folded Headings. Lines are one height, so only those in sight are built, as in List mode.

All Issues and Home #

All Issues is the Local copy: your Teams' Issues, and whatever a search has brought. Every other view is a Saved Search over it, My Issues (@me is:open) among them, put there once and Home to start with. Home is what Corkboard opens on, and g h goes back to. A filter also asks Linear for the same, translated to its IssueFilter, one page, 400 ms after it settles; a Saved Search marked Keep synced is asked again at every sync. Nothing else beyond your Teams is downloaded.

Filters and Search #

The typecase filter bar, read as Microfiche's :linear query is: @me project:Website,Billing +bug -priority:low status:"In Progress" due:<fri. Built so far: +label (or label:), state: (status:), is:started, priority: (p:), project: (in:), team:, cycle: (a number or name, current or none), estimate: (points, with < and >, or none), due:, created: and updated: with < and > (created:<-6m), - before any to leave it out, and words over the identifier, title and description. It reads only the Local copy, so it never waits on Linear. Saved Searches keep a Filter by name, as its text, in the settings file; + saves what's shown, ⌘1 to ⌘9 bring the first nine back, and My Issues clears the Filter. Link keys are named from the Issue's side and are fixed for now: is:blocked, is:blocking, has:subs, blocked-by:AUTH-12, blocking:, parent:, child:, related:, duplicate-of:. Parentheses are kept for nested Filters later (blocked-by:(project:Billing -is:done)), so no saved text may use them now.

⌘K is typecase's palette: every Command, by its menu's name, with its keys (menu or SPC). # searches Issues: the whole Local copy as you type, whoever's and however done, and after a pause in typing, Linear's own search, whose finds the Local copy lacks are listed under "from Linear" and kept in the Local copy for next time. An Issue the list has gets the cursor; one it doesn't is shown in the preview until the cursor moves.

One Search over titles, descriptions and comments, used three ways: jumping (⌘K, #), picking a verb's target, and words in the filter bar, with matching snippets in the preview.

Capture #

The Capture box, after Microfiche's: one line in the Filter's sigils, Fix token refresh +bug @sam p:"Q1 Platform" ^AUTH-12 >WEB-40 due:fri, with chips that read the line and write into it, and tab completing people, labels, Projects and Issues. c in the app, a global key through the menu bar extra, and corkboard capture …. It lands where it was captured from, or in the Account's default Team, shown as a chip to change. The app stays running with no windows, so it needs no separate helper as Microfiche's Capture agent is.

  • Built: c, Capture an Issue… in the File menu and the menu bar extra, ⌃⌥⌘C from any app (chosen in Settings, as the shortcut that brings Corkboard forward is), and corkboard capture …, which queues it for the app to send. The line reads team:, state:, p:, @, in:, +, due:, ^ (Parent), > or blocking:, blocked-by: and related:; tab finishes the one being typed and otherwise steps through the chips, Return makes it, and Escape forgets it. It's made from the Issue under the cursor's Team and the Project shown, or the Team chosen in Settings. A new Issue is a Change like any other, under an id Corkboard gives it, so Changes made before Linear has it name it too; it shows as CRK-new until Linear names it, and u takes it back, to Linear's trash once it's sent.
  • New Issue… (⌘N, and New Window is ⇧⌘N) is the same, in a window of its own with a title and a description over the line: tab goes from one to the next and ⌘↩ makes it. Escape puts it away keeping what's written. The toolbar has a button for it, and each Corkboard mode column a +, which starts it in that column.
  • A chip, clicked or reached with tab and Return, opens a picker under the chips: a search over what its field can be (Teams, statuses, priorities, people, Projects, Labels, dates, Issues), ↑ ↓ and Return to take one. Labels go on and off and the picker stays, since an Issue has several.

Welcome and the tour #

Red Pen's, on Typecase's welcome and tour. Welcome opens once, at a first launch with no Account, and again from Help: what Corkboard is, signing in, the keys worth knowing first, making Issues, and the menu bar. The tour points at the sidebar, the filter bar, the list, the preview, the modes and the toolbar, over made-up Issues: while it runs, every window shows a Local copy of the sample, made afresh each time, and ending it brings yours back. Syncing and sending Changes keep to yours throughout, and the edits to undo are forgotten when it starts and ends, so u never crosses from one to the other.

Stickies #

Notes of your own, which aren't Linear's (ADR 0006): a place in the sidebar, under All Issues, and g s. Each Sticky is a Markdown file in a folder you choose, its colour and Labels in a YAML header, so the folder can sit in iCloud Drive or git and any editor can write in it. Where each is pinned on the cork is the Local copy's, by its folder and name; a Sticky never moved goes in the next free place along a grid.

  • The three modes are the Issues': b pins them to the cork anywhere, each leaning a degree or two as a hand left it, dragged and left where they're dropped (Tidy the Board puts them back on the grid), with ← → and j k moving the cursor to the nearest one that way; a right click on bare cork puts a new one there. List mode is rows, and P writes them out under a Heading for each first Label.
  • There's no preview: a Sticky is written on in place, on its card, its row or its place on the paper, with its colours and Labels under the writing. e, ↩, a second click or a double one starts; Escape or ⌘↩ finishes. n (or ⌘N, while the Stickies are shown) makes one beside the cursor's, in its colour and first Label, ready to write on, and one made and left empty goes again. p c is its colour, p l its Labels, d tears it off to the Trash, and u undoes each of these, the typing included. Each Label has a colour of its own, picked by its name.
  • Tape (⇧N, or Tape a Section Here from a right click on the cork) is a strip of masking tape with a section's name handwritten on it, to head a part of the cork; it leans more than a note does. It's a Sticky of its own kind, so it's kept, written on, moved, torn off and undone as one is; List mode shows it as a heading, and Paper mode, which has its own Headings, leaves it out.
  • A checkbox written on one (- [ ] eggs) is ticked from the card itself, and the card and the row say 1/3. That's as far as doing goes: no states, dates or reminders. What needs a date is sent to Reminders; what's work, to Linear.
  • Holding the line on what a canvas could become: no shapes, drawing, images, templates, frames or live sharing for now. Frames (named areas) are the first that may come, once there are too many to read by Label.
  • The canvas (corkboard_core::canvas) is plain geometry over an R*-tree: what's in sight, what's under a point, what's beside the cursor, and where there's room. Only the Stickies in sight are built. The Issues' board keeps its own columns for now, and can move onto it once it needs what it does.
  • Microfiche's Notes and Apple Notes may become other folders of Stickies later, through the same seam, and a place showing more than one of them together would need an ADR of its own.

Writing #

Descriptions and comments are shown rendered as Markdown, as Red Pen's preview does, and written in Microfiche's Vim editor (microfiche-modal-editor): e the description, m a comment, @ and # completing people and Issues. Corkboard takes the editor from Microfiche's repository by tag at first, and it moves into typecase (typecase-vim, typecase-modal-editor) once Corkboard has shown what it needs changed.

Verbs #

Every verb has a key, a place in the leader, a menu item (which ⌘K lists) and a place in the right-click menu; the preview's fields are buttons for theirs. Built so far: s status, p 1–p 4 and p 0 priority, a assign, l p link to a Parent (refusing loops), l u take out from under it, u undo, l b blocks, l B blocked by, l r related, l d duplicate of, l x take a Link away, p p move to a Project. A picker is the palette's card, narrowed by typing. A Link made here waits as local-<Change> until Linear gives it an id.

Changes and sync #

See ADR 0001. Every edit is a Change: on screen at once, sent from a queue kept on disk, waiting while offline ("6 changes waiting"), taken back with a toast if Linear refuses. The last write wins on each field. u undoes your last edit, a bulk one included, by queuing the opposite Changes.

Accounts and signing in #

Several Accounts (ADR 0003), each a personal API key kept in the Keychain, with the Teams to follow and a default Team for Captures, chosen in Settings. Lists and Searches can span the Accounts of one Source, never two Sources; Links can't span Accounts.

Agents #

An MCP server (corkboard mcp) in the first version, over the Local copy: search, read, link, Nest, move into a Project, Capture. Lists sort by when Issues were made or last changed, and each Issue says both. An Issue's history (issue_history, corkboard history), who changed what and when, is the one read asked of Linear, since the Local copy keeps only what an Issue is now. Its edits are Changes like anyone's, so they queue, roll back and undo the same way. No agent panel yet: typecase's can follow.

corkboard mcp is a command of the app's own binary, not a server left running. It shares the Local copy with the app and sends Changes itself, so an agent's edits reach Linear with the app closed. Each sender takes a turn before sending, so none goes twice, and the app notices what another process wrote. The command line asks the same things (corkboard search, nest, link…), and a terminal Corkboard can stand on the same footing later, which is why what a Command changes is worked out in the core rather than in a view. An agent adds it as a stdio server, for Claude Code: claude mcp add corkboard -- /Applications/Corkboard.app/Contents/MacOS/corkboard mcp; Settings › Agent shows the setup for each client, and installs the corkboard command and a skill for it (skills/corkboard), for Claude Code and for the agents that read ~/.agents/skills, such as Codex and Pi; npx skills add finds it in the repository.

The Agent panel (typecase's) runs your own agent beside your Issues, as Red Pen and Microfiche do, given only that server. Its server also reaches the window it's in, along a socket of the window's own, to ask what it shows and to go to an Issue or a Filter, or keep a Filter as a Saved Search, as Microfiche's sidebar does. It opens on the conversation about all your Issues unless Settings says the one at the cursor. Its Changes say which agent made them. View › Ask the Agent sends Corkboard's prompts, and [[prompts]] in the settings file adds your own.
