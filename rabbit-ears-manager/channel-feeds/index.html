Channels apart from providers

Design agreed 2026-09-27, and built. Where building it settled something the design left open, it's noted under Settled while building.

An IPTV channel today belongs to one login: StreamChannel(source: .xtream(streamID:, provider:)), with its id derived from that pair and other logins' copies hanging off it in IPTVBackups. When a login goes away (activelion, 2026-09-26) its channels still play from a backup, but their guide, catch-up, Audit status and label all go dark with it, and the channel's number shifts whenever anything before it is hidden or removed.

This makes a channel a thing of its own — CNN on 42 — with an ordered list of equal feeds from any of your logins, and gives every channel a stored number.

Phase 1: stored channel numbers #

Stands alone; do it first.

  • Numbers are stored, fixed, and may have gaps, like real TV. One map, channel id → number, for the whole dial. channelOrder goes away; the dial is sorted by number. Moving a channel is setting its number; a taken number swaps.
  • Migration writes each channel's current position as its number, so the dial looks the same on day one.
  • Every channel gets a number the first time it's seen — built-ins, Pluto, Plex Live, the Tubi/Xumo catalog, Plex libraries — and keeps it for good.
  • A source that's unreachable (Plex server down, Pluto not answering, a login failing): its channels stay on the dial at their numbers and say so — "Plex server is down" — not a generic NO SIGNAL.
  • A channel the service has removed (service working, channel gone):
    • one you added (IPTV, saved Tubi…) stays on the dial, saying so, and is flagged in the Audit until you remove it;
    • an automatic one (Pluto, Plex Live) leaves the dial but its number stays reserved; it returns to it if it comes back. The Audit lists it — "gone, holding 57" — with Release.
  • Two devices numbering at once: the number map is merged per channel in SettingsSync (like flakySpells), not replaced. The same channel numbered twice keeps the lower number; two channels on one number, the one numbered first keeps it and the other takes the next free number. Every device settles on the same dial.
  • Hiding a channel leaves a gap; nothing shifts.

Phase 2: feeds #

The model #

  • An IPTV channel holds an ordered list of feeds, each (provider, streamID), plus tags read off the provider's name: region ("US") and quality ("HD", "4K", "RAW"). ChannelName.tidy already finds these and throws them away; it keeps them now.
  • Xtream feeds only for now, but the feed type leaves room for M3U, Pluto, Tubi or Plex feeds later.
  • Ids don't change. A migrated channel keeps xtream-<provider>-<streamID> as an opaque label: renames, hidden, buffers, flaky history and guide caches are all keyed by it. New channels take their id from their first feed, the same way.
  • Migration: the channel's own feed becomes #1, its IPTVBackups feeds #2…. IPTVBackups.channels is retired (its events stay until phase 3).

Playing #

  • Feeds in your order, skipping any whose login is full or down — today's IPTVLoad.ordered, for every channel.
  • The Select-button status names the provider actually playing ("via Strong").

Guide #

  • The first feed, in your order, whose provider has a guide for it; then the epg.pw fallback. The guide doesn't change with which feed is playing, so the Guide grid and the now/next banner agree.
  • ExternalGuides stays keyed per feed, so GuideFinder can find epg.pw guides for any feed, not just the first.

Earlier Shows #

  • Listed from the first feed with an archive.
  • Playing a show tries the same time window on each archived feed in order, skipping full logins (a timeshift URL is only stream + start + duration).
  • Manager setting, per channel: pin which feed's archive to use (e.g. trex's longer one for CNN).

Finding feeds (IPTVLinker) #

  • Still automatic: guide id, else name — now also requiring the region tag to agree, so "CNN" doesn't pair with "CNN International".
  • Only ever appends. A new login, or Find Again, adds feeds after the existing ones — to every channel, including ones you've edited. Several new feeds for one channel go in by quality, HD before SD.
  • Never undoes you. Your order stands; a feed you removed is remembered by provider and never comes back. (Replaces the per-channel "chosen" flag.)

Adding and merging #

  • Adding a channel from the Manager's IPTV search that's already on the dial offers "Add as feed to CNN (42)?" — the default — or a new channel.
  • Merge into… on a channel folds it into another: the channel merged into keeps its number, name, id and settings and takes the other's feeds at the end; the other is deleted and its number freed.

Removing a login #

  • Its feeds are dropped from every channel; the next feed becomes #1.
  • A channel left with no feeds stays on the dial showing it has none, flagged in the Audit, for you to relink or remove.
  • The sign-out warning reads "N channels lose a feed, M are left with none".

Flaky signals #

  • FlakySpells records which feed was playing. The Select-menu warning names the provider ("Strong's feed has been flaky tonight"); the Audit groups spells by provider.

Manager #

  • Each IPTV channel shows its feeds: drag to reorder, add, remove.
  • The Audit calls a channel dead only when all its feeds are.
  • Source label: "IPTV · Strong + trex".

What stays per channel #

Renames, buffer (ChannelBuffer), hidden.

Rollout #

  • The new lineup (channels with feeds, the number map) goes under new iCloud keys. All devices get the new build together.
  • The old keys (savedChannels, channelOrder, iptvBackups) are left as they were — a free backup if migration goes wrong — and removed in a later cleanup.

Phase 3: event channels #

The same pattern: an event channel's ordered list of (provider, categoryID) peers, replacing IPTVBackups.events, with its games matched across them as IPTVBackups.feeds(for:on:) does now.

Settled while building #

  • Settings channels (On Demand, TV Guide, Radio, Screensavers, Bars) have no stored number: they always follow the highest number, keeping them at the end of the dial. They can't be moved to a number, and no channel can be moved onto one.
  • Numbering starts once the whole lineup has loaded (your Plex servers included), so the dial is written down exactly as it was. Until then a device numbers 1…n as before.
  • Off the air lasts a week. A service that hasn't answered for 7 days has its channels leave the dial (numbers held) instead of staying on it saying so. What each service gave last is kept on each device (KnownChannels).
  • The number map merge: a later choice (a move, a release) wins; two first numberings keep the lower number; releases are remembered 30 days so they reach every device. See DialNumbers, with tests.
  • "Gone, holding" is per device. A channel only one device has (a Plex server the Mac can't reach, a playlist added on the Mac app alone) shows as gone on the others. Release it only if it's truly gone.
  • The Mac app's own search adds an IPTV channel as a new channel; the offer to add it as a feed of a channel you have is in the Manager (the Live TV pane). Search does leave out streams that are already a feed of a channel.
  • Add All in the Live TV pane skips channels you already have from another login, for adding as a feed one at a time.
  • Find Again on a channel also brings back the logins you took off it.
  • Removing a login can also remove the channels it leaves with no feeds, from the same confirmation.

Later, maybe #

  • M3U feeds. No connection count to check (treat as always free, or try last), usually no catch-up, guide from the playlist's url-tvg.
  • Feed ordering by quality. Flaky or slow feeds sink on their own. The per-feed flaky spells above are the data for it.
  • Per-channel guide pin, like the Earlier Shows pin.
  • Collections: rule-based groups across providers ("all news") in the Guide or the Select menu.
  • Logos: Xtream lists carry a stream_icon already, if the look wants them.
  • TMDB posters, synopses and cast for On Demand.
  • AI naming for the names ChannelName.tidy's rules miss.
