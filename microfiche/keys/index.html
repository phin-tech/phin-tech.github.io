Using Microfiche from the keyboard

Microfiche is modal, like Vim: keys move and restructure in Normal mode, and type in Insert mode. If you forget a key, SPC ? lists the common ones, and pressing SPC alone shows what each next key does.

This guide uses the Vim keys. Section 13 covers the Helix keys and changing single keys.

In this guide, SPC is the space bar, cmd becomes ctrl off macOS, and opt is the option key. C-o means ctrl-o.

1. Modes #

Status bar What keys do Get there Leave
NORMAL Move and act on text Where you start
INSERT Type i, a, A, o, O esc
VISUAL Select characters v esc
V-LINE Select whole lines V, or any text object esc
PLACE Choose where lines go m enter, m or esc
READING Move through the rendered Note SPC t r esc
LEADER Choose from the Leader menu SPC esc, or finish the sequence
COMMAND Type a : command or / search : or / enter or esc

2. Moving around #

Keys Moves
h j k l Left, down, up, right
w b Forward and back a word
0 $ Start and end of the line
gg G Top and end of the Note
[[ ]] Previous and next Heading, at any level
ctrl-cmd-up ctrl-cmd-down The same, and these also work while typing
/ n N Search, next match, previous match
cmd-f Search
:12 Go to line 12
za Fold or unfold here
gd Follow the Link at the cursor
cmd-click Follow the Link clicked, a web address in your browser. A plain click only moves the cursor
C-o Back from a Link

A number before a movement repeats it, so 3j goes down three lines and 2]] goes two Headings on.

A Heading jump skips Headings inside a fold, and the status bar says so when there are none left. In Insert mode it lands at the end of the Heading's line and you keep typing. It does nothing while Reading or Placing.

3. Editing #

Keys Does
i a Type at the cursor, or after it
A Type at the end of the line
o O Start a new line below or above
x Delete the character
D C Delete or change to the end of the line
dd cc yy Delete, change or copy the line
d, c or y, then a movement Act on what the movement covers, such as dw or y$
p P Paste after or before
u C-r Undo and redo
cmd-z cmd-shift-z Undo and redo
cmd-c cmd-x cmd-v System copy, cut and paste
cmd-a Select the whole Note
enter Follow a Link, change a Task's state, or open an Image, whichever the line holds
C-space while typing Open completion here

y, d, c, x and p share the system clipboard, as cmd-c and cmd-v do: yy here pastes in another app, and p pastes what another app copied. Clipboard under Editor in the settings window, clipboard = "vim", keeps them to Vim's own register instead. Lines copied from a Note reach other apps as Markdown, Tasks as checkboxes, - [ ] or - [x], and a cancelled one struck through, while pasting them back into Microfiche gives exactly what was copied. A word or two from inside a line is copied as typed.

Completion opens by itself where you're writing something it knows: a Task's state after [, a Field on a Task or the line under it, a Field's value after its :, a tag after + and an Event after @. Writing in Headings, bullets and paragraphs leaves it shut. C-space opens it anywhere it has something to offer. For long writing, Completion under Editor in the settings window can wait for C-space every time:

[editor]
completion = "on-demand"

For a chapter or an essay, tag a Heading's subtree +prose on the line under it, or put +prose at the very top to cover the whole Note. That's Writing: nothing in it is read as a Task or a Field, so it raises no diagnostics, stays out of the Agenda and opens no completion by itself. Its Headings still fold and show in the Outline, and it ends at the next Heading as deep as the tagged one or shallower.

# Chapter one
+prose
- a list here is just a list

A Markdown file, .md, is a Note that's Writing throughout. It's in the list of Notes, gf and search find it, and Links reach it and its Headings, but nothing in it is a Task. cmd-n makes one when the name you type ends in .md.

4. Selecting #

v selects characters and V selects whole lines. Move to grow the selection, press o to swap which end moves, and press esc to stop.

4.1 Text objects #

After v, or after d, c or y, these pick a piece of the Note. a takes all of it, with the blank lines after it. i takes only what's inside.

Keys Selects
ai The Item here: a paragraph, a list item with its children, a Block, or a Heading with its subtree
ah ih The nearest Heading above, with its subtree or just what's beneath it
ab ib The Block here, with or without its fences
ii What's beneath an Item's own line, or a paragraph's own text

So dah deletes a whole section, and yib copies a Block's contents.

4.2 Expanding a selection #

Press ai again while selecting and the selection widens one step each time: paragraph or list item, then its parent list item, then its Heading, then each parent Heading. - goes back a step. The status bar says nothing wider when you reach the top.

cmd-shift-up and cmd-shift-down do the same without pressing v first. The first press selects the Item at the cursor. They don't apply in Insert mode, where cmd-shift-up keeps its usual meaning of selecting to the top.

With something selected, d, c and y delete, change and copy it, and x deletes it too.

5. Moving Items #

An Item is a paragraph, a list item, a Block, or a Heading with its subtree. Moving one, or any selection, comes down to two steps: select it, then choose how far it's going.

A paragraph carries on over one blank line, so a thought can run to a few stretches of text and still move as one. Two blank lines end it, and so does a line holding only --, which shows nothing while Reading:

This stretch

and this one are one paragraph.


This is the next.
--
And so is this.

5.1 Select what you're moving #

To select Press
The Item at the cursor Nothing, moves use it by default
Exactly the lines you want V, then j or k
The Item, then what holds it cmd-shift-up until it's right, and cmd-shift-down to go back
The section you're in vah

5.2 Move it #

Going Press Then
Past the neighbour above or below [e or ]e Nothing, it moves at once
Somewhere else in this Note m j and k to find the spot, then enter, or esc to cancel
To another Note or Heading SPC i r Type to find it, then enter

For example, to move a section further down its Note, press vah, then m, then j until the line sits where you want it, then enter. To send a list item and its children to another Note, put the cursor on it and press SPC i r. Links to it in the folder follow it to its new Note.

u undoes a swap or a placement. The rest of this section goes through each way of moving in more detail.

5.3 Swap with a neighbour #

For small moves. It happens at once.

Keys Does
[e ]e Swap with the neighbour above or below
opt-up opt-down The same
<< >> Promote or demote
cmd-[ cmd-] The same

These act on the Item at the cursor, or on the selection. An Item only swaps with a neighbour of the same rank, so no neighbouring item to move past means there isn't one. > and < promote and demote while selecting.

5.4 Placing: choose a spot in this Note #

For moves where you want to see where it lands. A thin line shows the gap, and nothing changes until you confirm.

  1. Press m to pick up the Item at the cursor, or select lines first with V, ai, cmd-shift-up or similar and then press m. SPC i m does the same.
  2. Press j and k to step the Insertion line between Items. The view scrolls with it, and the lines you picked up stay tinted.
  3. Press enter or m to put them there, or esc to leave everything as it was.

A few things to expect:

  • A selection that starts with a Heading can only go before another Heading or at the end of the Note, so a section never lands inside a list.
  • A list item takes the indent of what it lands in front of. Placed just past the end of a list, it joins that list, above the blank line.
  • A paragraph keeps two blank lines from the prose around it, so they don't join, and one from anything else.
  • u undoes the whole move in one step.
  • Other commands and shortcuts do nothing until you finish.
  • A selection of part of a line counts as the whole Item at the cursor.

5.5 Refile: send it to another Note or Heading #

  1. Put the cursor on an Item, or select what you want to send. vah takes a whole section.
  2. Press SPC i r.
  3. Type to narrow the list of Headings, or start with / to choose a Note instead.
  4. Press enter.

It goes to the end of that Heading's subtree, or the end of that Note, and both Notes are saved. A selection moves exactly as it stands. The Note must be saved to a file and inside an open folder.

5.6 Changing what an Item is #

SPC i c, then h for a Heading, l for a list item, t for a Task, p for prose, c for a Callout or b for a code Block.

6. Tasks and dates #

Keys Does
enter or SPC m t Next state
SPC m x Done
SPC m w Waiting
SPC m c Cancelled
SPC m d s Set the Scheduled date
SPC m d d Set the Due date
SPC m d f Follow up: set the Follow-up date, which makes a todo or doing Task waiting
SPC m d t Insert a date
shift-right shift-left With the cursor on a date, a day later or earlier
shift-up shift-down With the cursor on a date, change the part it's on

The Date prompt takes shorthand and plain English, such as fri 3pm, +2w, nov 15 or in two weeks. Offsets count days, weeks, months and years from today (+3, +2w, +1m, +1y), or hours and minutes from now (+2h, +30min): m is a month, as in Org. Typecase's typecase-dates reads them, and its README lists every form.

A Follow-up date, follow:, is when to check back on something you're waiting on someone else for. Giving a todo or doing Task one makes it waiting, whether from SPC m d f, a Capture or a Subcommand, and typing one by hand does the same when you leave Insert mode, as its own undo step. enter still skips waiting. From its day the Task stays on today's Agenda, labelled "follow up", and it's never overdue. Setting a new one after its day has come is a chase, and adds a followed line to the Task's Log beneath it. A run of Log lines folds under the newest, and starts folded.

A Dependency, depends:, says a Task waits on another one. Type depends: on the waiting Task and completion lists the folder's Tasks by title, unfinished first. Picking one puts in its id:, and gives it one first if it has none, even in another Note, which is saved. The status bar names the Task when the cursor is on the Field, and while Reading it shows the title, with what each Task blocks. A Task is Blocked while any Dependency isn't done, and that never changes its state: the Agenda keeps it where its dates put it, labelled "blocked by" what blocks it. A cancelled Dependency still blocks, with a diagnostic asking you to decide, and so does a loop. An id found nowhere blocks nothing, with a diagnostic too.

Marking a Blocked Task done follows done_when_blocked under [tasks] in the config: "tell", the default, does it and says what blocked it, "ask" asks first, and "refuse" leaves it as it is. microfiche task and the MCP server follow it too, and under "ask" they take --anyway, or anyway, once you've said yes.

A Task's children are the Tasks nested beneath it: list items under a list item, and Tasks in a Heading Task's subtree. A Task with children shows how many are done, such as 2/3, while Reading and on the Agenda, where a cancelled child counts as done. With enforce_dependencies = true under [tasks], as Org's org-enforce-todo-dependencies, unfinished children block their parent. blocking:true or blocking:false on one Task overrides that for it. ordered:true on a Task makes its children go in order, each Blocked until those before it are finished, whether or not dependencies are enforced. A cancelled child or earlier sibling never blocks, because cancelling a step of your own is the decision a cancelled Dependency still waits for.

SPC f m Copy the MCP server's setup for an agent: j and k move, y copies

shift-enter on an Event in an agenda Notes on it in a meeting Note

The Launcher lists Commands, and the first character you type can change the list: # for Headings, / for Notes, ! for the Agenda and > for Commands, which is also where it starts. gf, cmd-o and SPC f f start it with / already typed; backspace returns to Commands. The Agenda also shows today's Events and the next week's from the Calendar app, once you allow it, and enter on one opens Calendar. Typing a Tag in either the Agenda or the Launcher's ! list keeps its date order and leaves out Events; backspace removes the Tag filter. In the Outline, tab goes into the selected Heading to show only what's beneath it. In a list of Headings, C-l inserts a Link instead of going there.

7.1 Writing a Query #

A Query is words that must all match, written as they are in a Note. The query box, a live list, an agenda Block, microfiche query and the MCP server all read it the same way.

Word Finds
todos Every Task. tasks is the same
open, overdue Tasks not finished, and those past their Due date
blocked Tasks with a Dependency that isn't done, so open -blocked is what can start now
todo doing waiting done cancelled Tasks in that state. is:done is the same
+work, tag:work Anything tagged, Tasks or not
due:<fri, due:>today, on:2026-11-14, follow:<today Due, Scheduled or Follow-up before, after or on a date, written as the Date prompt takes it: today, fri, +1w. One with a space goes in quotes, as due:<"10pm fri", and one with a time compares to the minute
area:ops Tasks with that Field
in:daily/ What's in Notes whose path starts that way
rent, "pay the rent" Words in the title, and a phrase in quotes kept together and in order
-done, !done, NOT done Leaves out what the word finds

For example, todos +work due:<fri -done lists unfinished work due before Friday. A Query saved in a Note keeps its words as written, so due:<fri always means the next Friday.

8. Changing the view #

Keys Turns on and off
SPC t r Reading, the rendered Note
SPC t p The Preview
SPC t i Inline Diagrams
SPC t n Relative line numbers
SPC t h The Heading indent
SPC t d Dark mode
SPC t a, cmd-j The Agent sidebar
SPC t A, cmd-shift-j The Agent sidebar on All Notes

SPC t t lists the appearances, System, Light and Dark, then every theme for light and for dark: Typecase, Gruvppuccin and Nightbrew Cove. Choosing one shows it at once and saves it in [theme] in the config file, so it lasts, and the Capture box shows it too. Saving the config file after changing [theme] by hand shows the change at once. SPC t d swaps light and dark for the session only, until an appearance is chosen.

The Heading indent shows each section 2 columns further in per Heading level, with its contents lined up beneath it. It's only how the Note looks, and the file doesn't change. While it's on, lines wrap slightly earlier in a deeply nested Note. To start with it on, set this in ~/.config/microfiche/config.toml, which SPC c f opens and which completes its sections, settings and values as you type. The same setting is in the settings window, under Editor:

[editor]
heading_indent = true

On a wide screen, max_width stops lines running across the whole window: they wrap after that many characters, or at the window's edge if it comes first. The Heading indent sits outside it, so indented lines hold as many characters as the rest. 0, the default, wraps at the window's edge.

[editor]
max_width = 100

Relative line numbers number each line by how far it is from the cursor's, so 5j or d3k can be read straight off the margin. The cursor's own line keeps its real number. To start with them on:

[editor]
relative_line_numbers = true

8.1 The Agent sidebar #

SPC t a or cmd-j slides the Agent sidebar out over the Note, from the right or the left as Side under Agent in the settings window says, with the cursor in its box: type, and enter asks. It's a coding agent of your own, Claude Code unless another is chosen there, run signed in as you already are and given only Microfiche's MCP server on the window's folder, so it reads and changes Notes as the server lets it and nothing else. Each Note has a conversation of its own, kept between runs, and All Notes has one for the folder as a whole. The agent can read the whole folder from any of them, so a Note's conversation can ask about everything too; it's only where the question is kept. The sidebar's title says which is showing, and the button beside it switches to the other. Opens on, under Agent in the settings window, chooses which a window's sidebar starts on, this Note or All Notes. Header switch there chooses how that button looks: Arrows, naming where it goes, Toggle, lit while All Notes is on, or Hidden, leaving cmd-shift-j. A Link in an answer opens its Note. Math in an answer, $…$ in a line or $$…$$ on its own, is typeset, and a mermaid code block is drawn as its diagram, in the theme's colours. Drag the sidebar's inner edge to widen it, and double-click the edge for its own width again.

Keys Does
SPC t a, cmd-j Slide it out, or back in
SPC t A, cmd-shift-j Switch between the Note's conversation and All Notes, which stays on until switched back. Slides it out on All Notes if it's away
esc Back to the Note, leaving it out
cmd-. Stop the agent answering

The agent's name in the sidebar's header is a menu of the agents too, and choosing one there changes the setting below, from the next message on. Under Agent, the settings window chooses the agent: Claude Code, Pi, oh-my-pi, Pi in Go, or an ACP agent: one from the ACP registry, which is installed into the app's caches the first time it's chosen, or one of your own, by its command line in ACP command, as ~/bin/agent --acp or gemini --acp. A path starts from / or ~, a bare name is found on your PATH, and quotes keep a part with spaces whole. Either way it's kept under [agent.acp] in the config file. The menus at the top of the sidebar choose its model and effort, before you ask anything: the agent is asked what it offers as the sidebar opens, which takes a moment for one spoken to over ACP. Each menu has a box to search by name or provider, with the provider beside each model. Model and effort under Agent in the settings window are the same menus. Either way they're kept in the config file under [agent.chosen], and a choice made before an agent starts is given to it as it does.

The agent works in the window as well as on files. It reads the Note on screen as the editor has it, unsaved edits and all, and what it writes there arrives as an edit that u undoes, left for you to save. It can ask where your cursor and selection are, write there, show you a Note, what a Query finds or the Agenda, and list the calendar's Events. A change it suggests rather than makes opens as a page of its diff:

Keys Does
enter Take the suggestion, as an edit that u undoes
esc, q Drop it
j k, ctrl-d ctrl-u, gg G Scroll

9. Files and windows #

cmd-, opens the settings window, a window of its own with a section for Appearance, General, Editor, Tasks, Git, Agent and Calendars. A change is saved in the config file as you make it, a typed one when you press enter or move away from it, and every window shows it at once, except that a window keeps its own SPC t h and SPC t n until the setting changes. A Reset button appears at the top of a section once something on it differs from its default. Keys of your own go in the config file's [keys] sections, which the window doesn't edit: its Config file section, SPC c f and the Microfiche menu open the file, and saving it changes the window too. The Capture key is read when the Capture agent starts.

:note path The same, typed. :note alone starts an untitled Note
cmd-shift-n, SPC f n, :new New window on the same folder, at its list of Notes, so Microfiche can sit in more than one Space. A Note is open in one window at a time: opening it from another brings the first forward. cmd-q closes every window, and one with unsaved changes stays open and says so

13. Helix keys, and your own #

Choose Helix for Keys under Editor in the settings window (cmd-,), or set keymap = "helix" under [editor] in the config file (SPC c f) and save it: the keys change at once. Helix selects first and acts after: w selects a word, and d deletes what's selected. With nothing selected, d deletes the character under the cursor. The Leader menu, Reading and the app's shortcuts are the same with either keymap.

Keys Does
h j k l Move, dropping any selection
w b Select the next word, or the one before
x Select the line; each press after takes in the next
v Select mode: moving stretches the selection. v or esc stops
; opt-; Collapse the selection, or flip its ends
% Select everything
opt-o opt-i Select the Item, then what holds it; go back a step
mi ma, then i h b Select inside or around an Item, Heading or Block
d c y Delete, change or copy the selection
i a Type before or after the selection
I A o O Type at the line's start or end; open a line below or above
p P Paste after or before the selection
u U Undo, redo
gh gl gg ge Line start and end; top and end of the Note
[h ]h Previous, next Heading
[e ]e < > Swap with a neighbour; promote, demote
M Start Placing
gd gf ctrl-o za / n N : As in Vim

To change single keys, with either keymap, add sections to the config file. Each mode that has its own keys has a section: normal, visual, reading and placing. A key names a command or a Leader action, and the names complete after the =. "script.name" runs the Script name, and "none" takes a key away.

[keys.normal]
"ctrl-s" = "file.save"
Q = "edit.delete-line"
g = { a = "cursor.top" }
x = "none"
"ctrl-t" = "script.today"

Keys are written as characters, or as space, esc, enter or backspace, with ctrl- or alt- in front for a chord. A sequence runs together, as gg, or comes apart, as g g. A key of your own replaces any sequence it would get in the way of, so binding g alone takes away gg. A key that can't be read, or names nothing, is left out, and the status bar says so. Insert mode, the command line and the Leader menu keep their keys.
